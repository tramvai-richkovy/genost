# SMT V Da'at Soundtrack Case Study

This note captures the research logic used to create the five GENOST draft projects in `../ost_drafts`. It is a creative brief, not a lore encyclopedia, and it should be used to design original soundtrack prompts rather than to copy Atlus music, melodies, or track structures.

## Canonical Lore Anchors

The central premise is useful for music because it is not just "desert apocalypse." The protagonist is pulled from modern Tokyo into Da'at, a ravaged alternate Tokyo occupied by demons, mythical deities, and tyrants. Aogami fuses with him into the Nahobino, a being neither fully human nor demon. This makes the musical point of view liminal: human city memory under supernatural pressure.

The story is driven by a dead or absent Creator, a power vacuum, Bethel's divine bureaucracy, and rival ideologies fighting over the fate of humanity. For music direction, that suggests non-heroic sacred scale: order, chaos, and survival should sound monumental, but not triumphant.

Vengeance adds a second story path, Canon of Vengeance, with Yoko Hiromine and the Qadistu. The official materials frame this as revenge by the fallen, new areas, new demons, new choices, and new music. For prompts, this supports a darker, more intimate, wounded tone than the base route: processed breath, revenge motifs, urban dread, and less stable resolutions.

Useful recurring terms:

- Da'at: ruined supernatural Tokyo, also conceptually tied to knowledge and mystic cosmology.
- Nahobino: fusion identity, human agency plus divine/demonic power.
- Bethel: angel-led organization trying to contain or govern the demon crisis.
- Magatsuhi: demonic/spiritual energy, useful as a cue for charged red/orange surges, transitions, and percussion bursts.
- Qadistu: Vengeance-route fallen demon faction, useful for bewitching, predatory, revenge-coded textures.

## Region Differentiation

The region guides identify the major Da'at regions as Minato, Shinagawa, Chiyoda, Taito, and, in Vengeance, Shinjuku. I used these as distinct environment briefs rather than as exact scene reconstructions.

### Minato: Starting Wasteland

Minato is the first region and sets the baseline: desert Tokyo, ruined roads, exposed sky, survival, and disorientation. The existing `smtv Sketch 01` already covered this: sparse 80 BPM dark ambient/downtempo, dusty pads, organic percussion, damaged synths, distant horn/guitar/choir color.

Creative translation:

- Base mood: mysterious, outworldly, haunting.
- Sound: tape hiss, low pads, old drum machines, organic percussion, roaring synth or guitar accents.
- Arrangement: lots of empty space, isolated motifs, slow filter movement.

### Shinagawa: Shelter In The Ruins

Shinagawa follows the initial wasteland with a different emotional temperature. The area list and in-game geography imply coastal/container/logistics remnants and a less purely desert-like space. I treated it as damp, corroded, and slightly more alive, with room for fairy-like refuge energy without making it cute.

Project: `smtv Sketch 02 - Salt Glass Shinagawa`

Creative translation:

- Base mood: humid, wary, luminous, wounded.
- Sound: glass pads, corroded container hits, distant water, prepared bells, muted guitar harmonics.
- Rhythm: downtempo industrial dub rather than open desert drift.
- Key/tempo: 96 BPM, A Phrygian for forward movement without battle urgency.

### Chiyoda: War Cathedral

Chiyoda is associated with government/business-center Tokyo geography such as Ginza and Otemachi, and the base story's escalation toward organized demon power. I translated it into a militarized civic ruin: infrastructure becomes ritual machinery.

Project: `smtv Sketch 03 - Iron Basilica Chiyoda`

Creative translation:

- Base mood: martial, ceremonial, infernal, authoritarian.
- Sound: industrial kick, pipe-organ synth, iron impacts, analog alarm brass, low choir, scraped guitar.
- Rhythm: straight machine march with polyrhythmic tom interruptions.
- Key/tempo: 132 BPM, C Phrygian for pressure and ritual severity.

### Taito: Late-Game Divine Maze

Taito is the later major region, with Ueno/Asakusa area references and a more open, circular navigation feeling. I treated it as less "road through ruins" and more "broken cosmology": a sacred maze with divine presences and unstable orientation.

Project: `smtv Sketch 04 - Godless Ueno Spiral`

Creative translation:

- Base mood: ethereal, ancient, exposed, reverent, unstable.
- Sound: organ synth, metallophone fragments, deep toms, bowed cymbals, wordless choir haze, frozen guitar halos.
- Rhythm: slow spiral pulse and asymmetric bell answers.
- Key/tempo: 88 BPM, E Locrian for late-game sacred instability.

### Shinjuku: Vengeance Urban Wound

PowerPyx and RPG Site distinguish Shinjuku as Vengeance-route content. I mapped it to the official Vengeance themes: fallen revenge, Yoko, Qadistu pressure, darker route logic, and expanded urban exploration. This track should feel more metropolitan and feverish than the base Da'at regions.

Project: `smtv Sketch 05 - Shinjuku Wound Array`

Creative translation:

- Base mood: bewitching, wounded, predatory, urban, feverish.
- Sound: fogged highrise pads, broken neon drums, distorted sub, processed wordless voice cuts, feedback guitar, rail-slice noise, prepared piano.
- Rhythm: heavy triplet lurch, industrial trip-hop rather than straight combat techno.
- Key/tempo: 108 BPM, G minor with triplet swing for a revenge-route pulse.

## OST-Inspired Musical Vocabulary

The official Vengeance site confirms expanded music and new locations. The RPGFan review is secondary criticism, not canon, but it gave useful vocabulary for prompt design:

- Da'at cues work as environmental/town themes for the netherworld version of Tokyo.
- The OST leans into boundary-pushing SMT tradition rather than clean fantasy scoring.
- The reviewer hears a fusion of older SMT elements with Southeast Asian, Mahayana Buddhist, and animist Indonesian traditions.
- Processed human voice is a recurring device: voice can become demonic, sacred, or synthetic.
- Many cues can sit on nonfunctional harmony or two-chord vamps instead of conventional progressions.
- Battle and late-game cues can rely on percussion, distortion, and processed instrumentation before becoming melodic.

Prompt vocabulary extracted from that:

- Nonfunctional vamp, Phrygian/Locrian pressure, unresolved minor-second tension.
- Wordless choir, formant blur, breath grains, vocal cuts, no lyrics.
- Ritual toms, asymmetric bells, metallophone fragments, struck pipe, bowed metal.
- Industrial techno, downtempo electronica, dark ambient, ritual minimalism, noise rock, post-rock restraint.
- Guitar as texture rather than riff: harmonics, feedback, scrapes, ebow halos.
- Synths as weather or architecture: glass pad, pipe-organ synth, fogged highrise pad, static veil.

## Secondary And Fan-Analysis Inputs

The following points are intentionally marked as interpretation. They came from secondary/fan-facing guides and criticism, then were converted into concrete prompt traits.

- RPGFan's OST review framed Da'at music as environmental/town scoring for the netherworld version of Tokyo. I used that to keep most tracks exploratory, loopable, and spatial instead of writing them like boss themes.
- RPGFan's discussion of older SMT tradition, altered voice, ritual references, and nonstandard harmony helped justify wordless choir grains, formant blur, nonfunctional vamps, and unresolved Phrygian/Locrian colors.
- RPG Site and PowerPyx helped confirm the practical region split used by players: Minato, Shinagawa, Chiyoda, Taito, and Vengeance-route Shinjuku. I used that fan/guide-facing structure as the suite map because it matches how a player experiences Da'at over time.
- Guide references to concrete districts such as Shinagawa, Ginza/Otemachi, Ueno/Asakusa, and Shinjuku helped translate lore into sonic scenery: container metal, civic war architecture, sacred maze geometry, and fogged nightlife ruins.
- None of this secondary material was treated as permission to copy tracks. It only shaped instrumentation, mood, texture, and arrangement density.

## Guardrails For Originality

These projects should be "inspired by" SMT V's broad atmosphere, not covers.

- Do not use reference audio from the game.
- Do not prompt for named SMT tracks or exact melodies.
- Avoid "in the style of Ryota Kozuka" as the primary instruction. Use concrete musical traits instead.
- Prefer region mood, instrumentation, rhythm, and arrangement logic.
- Keep prompts explicit: no lyrics, no copied motifs, no heroic fanfare, no festival EDM drops.
- Store research provenance in `commands.json` when a code agent seeds projects from web research.

## GENOST Mapping

The five-project suite should read as one soundtrack world with region contrast:

1. `smtv Sketch 01`: Minato-like starting desert, sparse and haunted.
2. `smtv Sketch 02 - Salt Glass Shinagawa`: damp ruins, container metal, shelter/fairy signals.
3. `smtv Sketch 03 - Iron Basilica Chiyoda`: war cathedral, industrial march, divine bureaucracy.
4. `smtv Sketch 04 - Godless Ueno Spiral`: late-game sacred maze, ancient/unstable vertical space.
5. `smtv Sketch 05 - Shinjuku Wound Array`: Vengeance-route urban wound, fallen revenge, triplet lurch.

For future project sets, the reusable method is:

1. Define a canon/lore axis.
2. Define a place/biome axis.
3. Define a musical vocabulary axis.
4. Convert those into structured GENOST composition fields.
5. Make blocks from functions, not aesthetics alone: atmosphere, pulse, bass, motif, human texture, organic/noise texture, transition FX.
6. Keep render durations below the MusicGen safe window.
